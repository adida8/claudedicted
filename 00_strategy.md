## 1. Niche framing — pick one

The job of the framing is to do two things at once: (a) explain in 6 words what someone gets by following, and (b) hold up over 12 months without dating. Below are 10 options, ranked by my read of fit against the brief. Top three are bolded.

### A. **Portfolio operator (with the non-developer hook)** ★ recommended
**Bio:** "Operating seven ventures with one AI co-pilot. (And I don't code.)"
Why it works: "portfolio operator" is professional enough to attract consulting inbound, documentary enough to support 70/30 angle, and survives if the venture count drifts. The parenthetical keeps the rare-combo hook live without making it the whole identity.
Why it might not: "operator" is jargon to non-operators — early on, audience self-selects slower.

### B. **The solo conglomerate** ★ strong alt
**Bio:** "One operator. Seven companies. AI is the org chart."
Why it works: owns a category nobody has claimed. "Solo conglomerate" is search-friendly and quotable. Sets up a multi-year narrative arc.
Why it might not: borrows weight from Berkshire/Tiny — invites comparison you may not want yet.

### C. **The non-developer founder** ★ strongest opening hook
**Bio:** "Non-developer. Seven businesses. One co-pilot."
Why it works: the rarest, sharpest hook in the brief. Audience-magnet for first 3 months.
Why it might not: locks identity to a deficit ("non-"). Dates if you ever pick up a little code. Strong as a *hook*, fragile as the *anchor*.

### D. The unfair cofounder
**Bio:** "I have an unfair cofounder. Watch us work."
Pros: intimate, builds parasocial trust fast.
Cons: positions Claude as the protagonist. Risky if you want consulting inbound — clients hire *you*, not the duo.

### E. Founder OS
**Bio:** "The operating system for running seven businesses at once."
Pros: framework-coded, consulting-bait.
Cons: implies a product. People will ask "where do I buy it?" within a month.

### F. Build in public, plural
**Bio:** "Most founders build in public. I build seven."
Pros: rides an existing genre while differentiating.
Cons: still inside the BIP sea — competing on volume.

### G. The AI-native operator
**Bio:** "Building companies the way they'll be built in 2030."
Pros: big-tent, future-facing.
Cons: pretentious unless heavily earned with proof.

### H. The new holdco
**Bio:** "A one-person holdco, run on AI."
Pros: borrows Tiny/Berkshire credibility.
Cons: direct comparison to billion-dollar holdcos isn't flattering at month 1.

### I. Operator > engineer
**Bio:** "Engineering used to be the moat. Operating is."
Pros: provocative, drives engagement.
Cons: argumentative — fights with the documentary angle.

### J. Seven hats
**Bio:** "Seven hats. One assistant. No engineers."
Pros: visual, memorable, easy on-camera frame.
Cons: cute. Hard to hold against the more durable options above.

**My pick: A (Portfolio operator with non-developer parenthetical).**
**My backup: B (Solo conglomerate).**
Either works; the rest of this doc reads either way.

## 3. Content pillars

Three pillars, mapped to post types. The mix lands at 75% builder-diary / 25% consulting signal — matches the locked 70/30 angle.

> One resolution call: the brief proposed 3 build-in-public Reels + 1 carousel + 1 opinion Reel = 5 posts/week, but also "~4 posts/week total." I locked the total at 4 by dropping one build-in-public Reel. Reason: 4/week is the sustainable shape for someone running 7 ventures, and the opinion Reel is non-negotiable for distribution. Easier to add a 5th later than to recover from over-commitment.

### Pillar 1 · The Work (2 Reels/week)
Screen-recording-led build-in-public. 30–60s. Format: 8s on-camera hook stating the problem → cut to screen recording of the actual work → end on the result.
- Tone: documentary. No tutorial voice.
- Constraint: real work, real ventures. No mockups, no recreations.
- Goal: trust through specificity.

### Pillar 2 · The Post-Mortem (1 carousel/week)
Long-form on one thing done that week. 6–10 slides.
- Slide 1: hook + venture name.
- Slides 2–4: what was the actual decision/problem.
- Slides 5–7: what was tried, what failed, what worked.
- Slide 8: what would change next time.
- Slide 9: soft CTA (bio link, or "DM me if you're working on something similar").
- Goal: "show your homework" content. This is what attracts consulting inbound.

### Pillar 3 · The Take (1 opinion Reel/week)
Contrarian or sharp observation about operating/AI/portfolios, backed by venture evidence.
- 30–45s.
- Format: "Everyone says X. Here's what I see working — [evidence from a real venture]."
- Goal: distribution engine. Opinion posts travel farther than process posts.
- Risk: opinion ≠ ranting. Each take must end on a piece of evidence from one of the 7 ventures.

**Why three not four.** Tempting to add a "lessons" or "tools I use" pillar. Don't. Four pillars dilutes the brand; three is the smallest stable number.

## 6. The 30-min/day workflow

The honest answer: 30 minutes *averaged* across the week, not literally 30 minutes every day. Daily posting is a trap (see §8), and daily *production* is a worse one. Here's the shape that actually fits 4 posts/week:

### Daily (3–5 min, every day you're working)
**Capture.** Every time you do something interesting across the 7 ventures — screen-record it. Always-on habit, not a creator task. Save raw clips to a single folder. Title them: `YYYY-MM-DD · venture · 4-word description.mov`.

### Sunday batch (60–90 min, once a week)
**Pull 4 captures → edit → write captions → schedule.**
1. Open the capture folder. Pick 4 clips that map to next week's calendar slots.
2. Edit each Reel: 8s on-camera hook + screen recording + result frame. Use one preset.
3. Write captions: 2-line hook + 3-sentence body + soft CTA. No hashtag spam.
4. Build the Friday carousel: pick one pull from the week's work, fill the slide template.
5. Write the Sunday opinion Reel script. 80 words max.
6. Schedule all four. Done.

### Post-day rhythm (8 min, four times a week)
**On post mornings (Mon/Wed/Fri/Sun):**
- 5 min before posting: leave thoughtful comments on 5 accounts in your target niche. This warms the algorithm and pulls eyeballs to your post.
- Post.
- 3 min later, reply to the first 3 comments on your post.

### DM hygiene (5 min, once a day)
- Reply to any inbound. Save standout messages as fodder for future posts (anonymized).
- Don't pitch consulting. Let people ask. They will.

### Total weekly time
- Capture: ~20 min (passive, baked into your day)
- Sunday batch: 90 min
- Post-day rhythm: 32 min (8 × 4)
- DM: 35 min (5 × 7)
- **Total: ~3 hours/week.** Maps to 30 min/day if you average it, but the shape is "one big block + small daily touches."

> If you want literal 30-min/day, the version above is the only one that survives contact with running 7 businesses. The naïve interpretation — film, edit, post in 30 min daily — collapses by week 3.

## 7. Visual & aesthetic guidelines

Lock these on day 1 so you stop deciding them every post.

### Palette
- One accent color. Pick now. Suggested: deep electric blue (#1E40FF) or burnt orange (#E25A1C). Avoid black-and-white only — too austere for documentary tone.
- White + accent + one neutral grey. That's it.

### Typography
- One sans-serif. Inter or Söhne or SF Pro. Pick one, use it on every thumbnail, every carousel, every caption overlay.
- Headlines: bold weight, max 6 words.

### Reel thumbnails
- Face (chest-up) on the left third, 4-word headline on the right two-thirds.
- Same template every Reel. No exceptions. Consistency > creativity here.

### On-camera framing
- Chest up. Eye-level camera. Even light from a window or one panel.
- Neutral background — not a curated bookshelf. Plain wall is better.
- Don't move while talking. Static frame = serious.

### Screen recordings
- Zoom to 125%+ before recording. Mobile readability is the constraint.
- Cursor highlight on.
- No "let me explain" voiceovers in the screen rec — the captions do that.

### Carousels
- 9-slide max. 8 is the sweet spot.
- Slide 1: hook + venture name in the corner. Slides 2-N: one idea per slide, big text, lots of whitespace.
- No screenshots of long blocks of code or copy. If it doesn't read at arm's length on a phone, redesign it.

### Captions
- Line 1: hook (echoes the Reel hook).
- Lines 2–4: 3-sentence body.
- Line 5: soft CTA — "DM me if you're running into this" or "saved this from the [venture] log".
- 3 hashtags max. Pick a stable set: #portfoliooperator #buildinpublic #aiops (or your equivalents).

## 8. Anti-patterns — explicit DO-NOTs

**Don't teach Claude.** Show Claude being used. The audience that converts isn't beginners.

**Don't post daily.** Cadence is 4/week. Daily produces lower-quality content and burns out the operator. The brief is right about this.

**Don't make "how to use AI" content.** That market is saturated and the audience is the wrong one (beginners, not operators).

**Don't hide the failures.** Show the cul-de-sacs. Documentary > highlight reel. Trust comes from the misses.

**Don't polish on-camera into corporate.** Imperfect = trustworthy. Slight stumbles stay in.

**Don't post and ghost.** 5 min of engagement *before* posting, 3 replies *after*. Non-negotiable.

**Don't niche down too fast.** First 90 days, spread across the 7 ventures. Then narrow if one clearly wins audience attention.

**Don't sell consulting in captions.** Bio + DM only. Let the work create the inbound.

**Don't do "day in the life" formats.** They drift to lifestyle and away from the work.

**Don't fabricate work to fit the calendar.** If a week is thin, swap slots or skip. One missed post will not kill the account. A staged post will.

**Don't argue with critics in comments.** Reply once, with substance, then disengage.

**Don't cross-post Reels to LinkedIn natively.** Reels read as low-effort there. Cross-post the *carousels* and the *opinion Reels* (re-framed as a short text post), not the build-in-public Reels.

## 9. 90-day milestones (so you know if it's working)

Targets are deliberately modest — this niche is small and the audience is high-value, not high-volume.

| Metric | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posts shipped | 16 | 32 | 48 |
| Followers | 500 | 1,500 | 4,000 |
| Avg Reel views | 1,500 | 5,000 | 15,000 |
| Inbound DMs of substance | 2 | 6 | 15 |
| Consulting conversations | 0 | 1 | 3 |

**Read the metrics that matter.** Followers are vanity until ~3,000. DMs and consulting conversations are the real signal. If you're at day 60 with 1,500 followers and 6 substantive DMs, you're winning. If you're at 5,000 followers with zero DMs, you're entertaining the wrong audience.
